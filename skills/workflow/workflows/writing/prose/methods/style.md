# Unslop

22. Answer directly rather than opening with praise or agreement.
23. Cut filler: "in order to" becomes "to".
24. Keep necessary uncertainty; remove stacked hedges.
25. End with specific facts or plans rather than a generic conclusion.
26. Replace abstract metaphors such as "substrate", "nexus", "scaffolding", "ratchet", or "north star" with the concrete concept. Preserve a term when it is the actual technical name.
27. Say what it does, not how it feels. Name the mechanism or number; cut claims that could describe any project.
28. Keep one idea per sentence and split sentences that need rereading.
29. Prefer active voice; name the actor unless unknown or irrelevant.
30. Replace vague adverbs with a stronger verb or a measured number.
31. Use the everyday word: "use", not "utilize" or "leverage".
32. Prefer literal phrasing to metaphors, aphorisms, or personified code.
33. Write complete sentences rather than fragments and symbol-speak. Keep actual code, paths, diagrams, and syntax intact.
